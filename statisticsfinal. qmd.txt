This project uses a dataset on video game habits among participants over a one-month period. The goal is to examine how much time people spend playing video games on weekdays compared to weekends and whether there is a statistically significant difference between them.


The dataset includes the following variables:

- `participant_id`: A unique identifier for each participant (categorical)
- `weekday_playtime`: Total average playtime on weekdays in hours (numeric)
- `weekend_playtime`: Total average playtime on weekends in hours (numeric)
- `age`: Age of participant (numeric)
- `gender`: Gender of participant (categorical: Male, Female, Other)

Research Questions

1. How does video game playtime compare between weekdays and weekends?
2. Is there a correlation between age and average weekend playtime?

Boxplot of Weekday vs. Weekend Playtime
{r}
library(tidyr)
library(ggplot2)

long_data <- data %>%
  pivot_longer(cols = c(weekday_playtime, weekend_playtime), 
               names_to = "day_type", 
               values_to = "hours_played")

ggplot(long_data, aes(x = day_type, y = hours_played, fill = day_type)) +
  geom_boxplot() +
  labs(title = "Video Game Playtime: Weekdays vs. Weekends", x = "Day Type", y = "Hours Played") +
  scale_fill_manual(values = c("skyblue", "lightgreen")) +
  theme_minimal()


Summary Statistics
{r}
library(dplyr)

data %>%
  summarize(
    mean_weekday = mean(weekday_playtime, na.rm = TRUE),
    sd_weekday = sd(weekday_playtime, na.rm = TRUE),
    mean_weekend = mean(weekend_playtime, na.rm = TRUE),
    sd_weekend = sd(weekend_playtime, na.rm = TRUE)
  )

Paired Sample T-test
We use a paired t-test since each participant has both weekday and weekend playtime data.
{r}
t.test(data$weekday_playtime, data$weekend_playtime, paired = TRUE)


Correlation: Age vs. Weekend Playtime
{r}
CopyEdit
cor.test(data$age, data$weekend_playtime)


Conclusion:
In this project, we analyzed a dataset of participants' video game habits to explore how playtime differs between weekdays and weekends. Descriptive statistics showed that weekend playtime tends to be higher, with a wider spread. Visualizations including histograms and boxplots helped illustrate this difference clearly.
Using a paired sample t-test, I found a statistically significant difference in playtime between weekdays and weekends, suggesting that people play significantly more on weekends. Additionally, a correlation test showed a weak negative correlation between age and weekend playtime, indicating that age may have a minor influence on weekend gaming habits. These findings highlight behavioral patterns that may be useful for game developers or digital wellbeing studies.
